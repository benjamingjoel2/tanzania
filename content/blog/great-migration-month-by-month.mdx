Each year, well over a million wildebeest, together with hundreds of thousands of zebra and gazelle, move in a great loop through the Serengeti ecosystem and into Kenya's Maasai Mara. They are following the rains and the fresh grazing that comes with them, and lions, hyenas, cheetahs and crocodiles follow them in turn.

The [Great Migration](/experiences/great-migration) is not a single event but a continuous cycle. There is no one best month; there is only the best month for what you want to see. This guide sets out where the herds usually are throughout the year.

> A word of caution: the migration follows the rain, not the calendar. The pattern below holds in most years, but the timing can shift by weeks. Good planning means choosing camps that give you options.

## The year at a glance

| Month | Where the herds usually are | What to expect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| January | Southern Serengeti and Ndutu | Herds settle on the short-grass plains; calving begins |
| February | Southern Serengeti and Ndutu | Peak calving; intense predator activity |
| March | Southern and south-central Serengeti | Herds start to disperse; long rains approaching |
| April | Central Serengeti, moving north-west | Long rains; green landscapes, few visitors |
| May | Central Serengeti and Western Corridor | Rutting season; long columns on the move |
| June | Western Corridor | Grumeti River crossings possible |
| July | Western Corridor to northern Serengeti | First Mara River crossings possible late in the month |
| August | Northern Serengeti and Maasai Mara | Peak river crossing season |
| September | Northern Serengeti and Maasai Mara | Crossings continue in both directions |
| October | Northern Serengeti | Final crossings; herds begin to turn south |
| November | Eastern and central Serengeti | Short rains; herds stream south |
| December | Southern Serengeti | Herds arrive on the plains; quieter season |

## Calving season: December to March

When the short rains fall in November and December, the herds move onto the short-grass plains of the southern [Serengeti](/destinations/serengeti) and the area around Ndutu, on the edge of the Ngorongoro Conservation Area. The grasses here grow on volcanic soils rich in minerals, ideal for nursing mothers.

From late January through February, hundreds of thousands of calves are born within a few weeks. A wildebeest calf can stand within minutes and run with the herd soon after. The plains are crowded with life, and predators are never far away. Cheetah, in particular, thrive on the open country.

This is one of our favourite times to be in the Serengeti. The landscape is green, the light is beautiful and the wildlife viewing is outstanding. Our [calving season safari](/safaris/calving-season-safari) is built around it.

## The long rains: April and May

As the southern plains dry out, the herds begin to move north and west. April and May bring the long rains, and many camps close for the season. Those that remain open offer real value and a quieter, greener Serengeti.

In May, the rut is in full swing. Males compete noisily for territory and females, and long columns of wildebeest can stretch to the horizon as the herds move through the central Serengeti.

## The Western Corridor: June and July

By June, many of the herds have reached the Western Corridor, a long finger of the park that stretches towards Lake Victoria. Here they must cross the Grumeti River, home to some very large crocodiles. Crossings here are less well known than those on the Mara, but they can be dramatic, and there are fewer vehicles.

## River crossings: July to October

From around July, the herds reach the northern Serengeti and the Mara River. The crossings that follow are the most famous scenes of the migration: thousands of animals massing on the bank, hesitating, and then plunging into the water.

Crossings are impossible to predict. The herds can gather for hours and then turn away, or cross in minutes without warning. Patience is essential, and so is a good guide who knows the crossing points and when to wait.

We favour camps in the Kogatende and Lamai areas, close to the river, and recommend at least three nights to give yourself a realistic chance. Our [migration river crossings safari](/safaris/migration-river-crossings) is designed for exactly this.

### Tips for crossing season

- Book early. The best camps in the north fill a year or more in advance.
- Allow time. Three or four nights in the north is far better than two.
- Stay flexible. Crossings happen on the herds' schedule, not ours.
- Bring a long lens if you are a photographer, and plenty of memory cards.

## The return south: November

When the short rains arrive in November, the herds turn south again, moving through the eastern and central Serengeti towards the southern plains. It is a quieter time with fewer visitors, and the wildlife can be excellent as the herds pass through.

## Choosing your timing

The right month depends on what matters most to you.

1. **For calving and predators**, travel from late January to February.
2. **For river crossings**, travel from mid-July to September.
3. **For value and solitude**, consider November, or the shoulder months of March and June.

Whenever you travel, the Serengeti has plenty to offer. Resident wildlife remains throughout the year, and the central Seronera area is excellent in any month. Pairing the Serengeti with the [Ngorongoro Crater](/destinations/ngorongoro-crater) adds a superb second highlight.
