There are few better ways to see Tanzania than to combine the wildlife of the safari parks with the warm water and white sand of the coast. The two halves complement each other perfectly: early starts and long days in the bush, followed by time to rest, swim and reflect.

Tanzania makes this easy. Short flights link the safari airstrips directly with Zanzibar, so you do not need to backtrack or spend a day in transit. Here is how to plan it well.

## How long do you need?

For a first trip, ten to fourteen days is ideal. A typical balance might be:

- **Six to eight nights on safari**, perhaps in Tarangire, the Ngorongoro Crater and the Serengeti.
- **Four to five nights on the coast**, on Zanzibar or one of the quieter islands.

With less time, you can still make it work. A week could combine three or four nights in the Serengeti with three nights on Zanzibar. With more time, add a second safari area or a few nights in Stone Town before the beach.

> As a rule, give the safari the larger share. You can always relax at home; you cannot always come back to the Serengeti.

## Which order works best?

We almost always recommend the safari first. Game drives begin early, and you will want your energy for them. Ending on the beach gives you time to unwind before the journey home, and to let everything you have seen settle.

There is one practical point in favour of this order too. Light aircraft have strict luggage limits, typically around 15 kg per person in soft-sided bags, including hand luggage. Your beach clothes do not weigh much, but if you are carrying more, most hotels can store excess luggage while you are on safari.

## Choosing your safari

The northern circuit is the natural partner for Zanzibar. A classic route begins in Arusha, then visits [Tarangire](/destinations/tarangire) for elephants, the [Ngorongoro Crater](/destinations/ngorongoro-crater) and the [Serengeti](/destinations/serengeti), with a flight from a Serengeti airstrip to Zanzibar at the end.

If you have been on safari before, the southern parks are an excellent alternative. [Nyerere](/destinations/nyerere) is only a short flight from Zanzibar, and its boat safaris on the Rufiji River make a lovely contrast to game drives.

## Choosing your coast

Zanzibar is larger and more varied than many people expect, and where you stay matters.

### Stone Town

The old town of Zanzibar City is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, a maze of narrow alleys, carved wooden doors, markets and mosques that tells the story of centuries of trade across the Indian Ocean. One or two nights here, with a guided walk and a [spice tour](/experiences/zanzibar-spice-tours), add depth to a beach stay.

### The north

Nungwi and Kendwa, at the northern tip, are less affected by the tides than the east coast, so swimming is possible for most of the day. The sunsets are beautiful, and the atmosphere is livelier.

### The east coast

Paje, Jambiani, Matemwe and Michamvi have long, white beaches and turquoise lagoons. The tides here are large: at low tide the sea can retreat a long way, revealing seaweed farms and rock pools. It is beautiful, but if swimming at any time of day is important to you, choose a hotel with a pool or consider the north.

### The quieter islands

For more seclusion, consider [Pemba Island](/destinations/pemba-island), known for its diving and green, hilly interior, or [Mafia Island](/destinations/mafia-island), with its marine park and seasonal whale sharks. Both are quieter than Zanzibar and have only a small number of places to stay.

## When to go

The best time for a safari and Zanzibar trip is during the dry seasons:

1. **June to October** is excellent for wildlife across the country, with the river crossings in the northern Serengeti and dry, pleasant weather on the coast.
2. **Late December to February** combines calving season in the southern Serengeti with hot, sunny days on Zanzibar.

The long rains, usually from late March to May, bring heavy showers to both the parks and the coast, and some lodges close. November brings the short rains, which tend to be lighter and shorter.

## Practical points for Zanzibar

A few things are worth knowing before you go.

- **Dress modestly in Stone Town and villages.** Zanzibar is predominantly Muslim; cover shoulders and knees away from the beach.
- **Be aware of Ramadan.** During the holy month, avoid eating or drinking in public during the day. Hotels operate as normal, though some restaurants may close.
- **Check the entry rules.** Zanzibar has its own requirements for visitors, including travel insurance, so please check current rules on official government sources before you travel.
- **Plan for health.** Malaria is present on Zanzibar as well as the mainland, so take advice from a travel health clinic.
