Tanzania is a big country, roughly four times the size of the United Kingdom, and one of the most rewarding in Africa for travellers. Within its borders are the plains of the Serengeti, the highest mountain on the continent, chimpanzee forests on the shores of Lake Tanganyika and a coastline of coral reefs and historic trading towns. The challenge is rarely finding something worth seeing. It is deciding what to leave for next time.

This guide sets out the main regions, what each does best and how they fit together.

## The northern circuit

For most first-time visitors, the northern circuit is the heart of a Tanzanian safari. The parks lie within a few hours' drive of one another, roads and flights link them easily, and the variety of landscape and wildlife is remarkable.

### The Serengeti

The [Serengeti](/destinations/serengeti) is the country's most famous park, and with good reason. Its open plains support some of Africa's highest densities of predators, and it is the stage for the [Great Migration](/experiences/great-migration), as well over a million wildebeest, along with zebra and gazelle, follow the rains in a year-long circuit. The central Seronera area is excellent all year, while the south comes into its own during calving season and the north during the river crossings.

### The Ngorongoro Crater

The [Ngorongoro Crater](/destinations/ngorongoro-crater) is a vast collapsed volcano whose floor holds lions, elephants, buffalo and one of the few places in Tanzania where you have a realistic chance of seeing black rhino. Descending from the forested rim at dawn is one of the great safari experiences. Visits to the crater floor are limited to a set number of hours, so we plan an early start to enjoy it before the day warms up.

### Tarangire and Lake Manyara

[Tarangire](/destinations/tarangire) is the place for elephants. In the dry season, large herds gather along the Tarangire River beneath ancient baobab trees, and the park is often quieter than its neighbours. [Lake Manyara](/destinations/lake-manyara), set beneath the Rift Valley escarpment, is smaller but lovely, with groundwater forest, flamingos in season and excellent birdlife.

> The northern circuit rewards time. Three nights in the Serengeti is good; four or five lets you settle into the rhythm of the bush.

## Kilimanjaro and Arusha

At 5,895 m, [Kilimanjaro](/destinations/kilimanjaro) is the highest mountain in Africa and the tallest free-standing mountain in the world. No technical climbing is required, but altitude is a serious challenge and the choice of route matters. Our guide to [choosing a Kilimanjaro route](/blog/how-to-choose-a-kilimanjaro-route) explains the options, and we usually recommend longer itineraries such as [Lemosho](/kilimanjaro/lemosho) for the best chance of success.

[Arusha](/destinations/arusha) is the gateway to the north. Many travellers pass through quickly, but a night on a coffee farm in the foothills of Mount Meru is a gentle way to begin a trip, and Mount Meru itself is a superb trek.

## The southern parks

If you have been on safari before, or you value solitude above all, look south. [Ruaha](/destinations/ruaha) is a wild landscape of baobabs, rocky hills and sand rivers, with large lion prides and a real sense of remoteness. [Nyerere](/destinations/nyerere), one of the largest protected areas in Africa, is defined by the Rufiji River, and offers a rare combination of game drives, walking and boat safaris.

The southern parks receive far fewer visitors than the north. You may see only a handful of other vehicles in a day, and the guiding culture places a strong emphasis on walking. Our [southern wilderness safari](/safaris/southern-wilderness) brings the two parks together.

## The western forests

On Tanzania's far western border, the [Mahale Mountains](/destinations/mahale-mountains) and [Gombe Stream](/destinations/gombe-stream) protect forests that are home to wild chimpanzees. Gombe is where Jane Goodall began her research in 1960; Mahale is larger, more remote and reached by light aircraft and boat. Both sit on the shores of Lake Tanganyika, whose clear water is ideal for swimming and kayaking.

These are not easy places to reach, and that is part of their magic. A few nights in the west pair well with the Serengeti, as in our [chimpanzees and Serengeti journey](/safaris/chimpanzees-and-serengeti).

## The coast and islands

No trip to Tanzania feels complete without time on the Indian Ocean.

- [Zanzibar](/zanzibar) offers the greatest variety, from the alleys and carved doors of Stone Town to beaches on every coast, spice farms and excellent snorkelling.
- [Pemba Island](/destinations/pemba-island), to the north, is green, hilly and far quieter, with dramatic diving along the Pemba Channel.
- [Mafia Island](/destinations/mafia-island), to the south, is protected by a marine park and known for its seasonal whale sharks.

A short flight links the safari parks with the coast, so a [safari and Zanzibar](/safaris/safari-and-zanzibar) combination is easy to arrange.

## Culture and people

Tanzania's people are as much a part of a journey as its wildlife. In the north, the Maasai graze cattle across lands they share with wildlife. Near [Lake Eyasi](/destinations/lake-eyasi), the Hadzabe maintain a way of life based largely on hunting and gathering. We arrange only visits that communities host themselves, with fair payment going directly to the people involved.

## Putting it together

A first trip of ten to fourteen days might combine Tarangire, the Ngorongoro Crater and the Serengeti with a few days on Zanzibar. A second trip might focus on Ruaha and Nyerere, or the chimpanzee forests of the west. Climbers often add a short safari after Kilimanjaro, which is a welcome way to rest tired legs.

When deciding where to go, it helps to consider:

1. **The month you can travel.** Seasons shape everything, from the migration to the tides on Zanzibar's east coast.
2. **Your appetite for travel time.** Remote areas usually mean more flights, but also more solitude.
3. **What you most want to remember.** A single great encounter, a summit, a few quiet days by the sea, or all three.

Your specialist will help you weigh these up and design a route that fits. When you are ready, [start planning your journey](/plan-your-journey).
